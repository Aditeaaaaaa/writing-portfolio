## 1. Why does my internet keep dropping during video calls? 📶

*Audience: small business owners and aged care services. Format: troubleshooting guide.*

If you run a small business or aged care service, you've probably dealt with this: staff are frustrated, clients call back again and again, and your reliability takes a hit.

I used to work in telecommunications. Here's what's actually happening and how to fix it.

### Why it happens

Your router shares bandwidth (your internet speed) across every device connected to it. During busy periods, like video calls, background downloads and streaming, that speed gets stretched thin. Video calls need a stable connection, so they suffer first.

### Two fixes that solve most problems ✅

**1. Use an Ethernet cable**
A physical cable from your laptop straight to the router is far more stable than WiFi, especially in busy offices.

**2. Set up QoS (Quality of Service)**
This tells your router to put specific devices first during work hours, so your video call always gets bandwidth first.

These two changes fix most dropout issues for most small businesses. 💡

## 2. Where is "the cloud", really? ☁️

*Audience: anyone new to tech. Format: plain-English explainer.*

When I first heard the term "cloud computing", I thought it was just a fancy way of saying online storage.

Photos? Cloud. Documents? Cloud. That was pretty much the extent of my understanding.

What confused me wasn't the technology itself. It was the physical side of it:

- Where *is* the cloud?
- How big are these data centres?
- If thousands of companies use the same cloud provider, how does privacy work?
- Can anyone access my data?

The more I learned, the more I realised the cloud isn't some magical thing floating around on the internet. It's real infrastructure. Real buildings. Real servers. Real people maintaining them.

What finally made it click was thinking about it like a business owner. If you're a small business, does it make more sense to spend a fortune on servers, hardware, security, power, cooling and backups? Or to rent the infrastructure you need and focus on growing your business?

That's when cloud computing started making sense to me:

> **You're not buying the infrastructure. You're buying access to it.**

Sometimes the best way to understand technology isn't memorising definitions. It's asking the questions that seem obvious.

## 3. More signal bars, more speed: how 5G adapts to your signal 📡

*Audience: curious phone users. Format: short explainer with a simulation video.*

Something I always found interesting from my time in telecom: more signal bars doesn't just mean a "stronger" signal. It changes how much data your phone can send at once.

Near the tower, the signal is clean, so the network uses **256-QAM** and fits **8 bits into every symbol**. As you move away and noise builds up, it steps down to simpler schemes like **16-QAM** or **QPSK**. Slower, but the connection stays alive.

In my simulation, you can see the dots spread out as the signal gets weaker, and the speed drops with it.

## 4. I finally looked up what Jira is (and no, it's not the spice) 🫚

*Audience: people new to workplace tools. Format: learning-in-public post.*

As an Indian, I have to get this joke out of the way first: every time someone said "Jira" at work, a small part of my brain heard "jeera", like the cumin seeds in my kitchen spice rack.

Humour aside, I honestly thought Jira was a coding tool that only developers touched. So I finally looked it up.

### What Jira actually is 💡

It's not just for coders. It's a shared online space where teams manage projects together: boards, tasks and timelines, all in one place.

### What impressed me ✨

- **Personalisation:** you can add photos and make the space feel less like a spreadsheet and more like your team's space.
- **Rovo (Jira's AI):** specialised AI for specific tasks, so nothing gets left behind.
- **Automation:** quiet background rules that do the busywork, so people don't have to.

### Why I wrote this 📝

If you've heard "Jira" thrown around and felt that quiet "I probably should know this" feeling: you're not behind. Curiosity doesn't need a reason to wait. Sometimes you just look it up.
